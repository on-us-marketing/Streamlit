# LinkedIn Style Guide

Last updated: July 2026
Status: Draft for review
Use stage: Repurposing only

## Purpose

Use this file to turn a master content brief into a LinkedIn-ready marketing distribution asset.

The LinkedIn output should be easy for a marketer to review, lightly edit, and paste into LinkedIn after final human approval. It should not be an outline, markdown note, or internal review memo.

LinkedIn should attract attention, communicate one useful marketing message, and create a reason to engage with On-us. It is an awareness and marketing-distribution asset, not consideration-stage collateral, a compressed Blog, an SEO FAQ, or an academic essay.

Always load and read `brand_voice.md` in full before LinkedIn repurposing. Treat it as the highest-priority LinkedIn style instruction. It controls the preferred On-us voice, reading weight, hook patterns, sentence rhythm, emoji use, format rotation, and CTA patterns.

## Marketing Role

The LinkedIn post should:

- Establish the hook, market signal, buyer problem, or milestone in the first 1-2 lines.
- Keep the strongest and most quotable message within the first 210 characters.
- Focus on one central message rather than summarising every section of the master content.
- Make the target buyer and business relevance clear.
- Connect the topic to On-us through a product mechanism, point of view, case, or milestone.
- Present no more than 2 concrete buyer benefits when a list is genuinely useful.
- Use no more than one proof point and one short product-mechanism sentence.
- Leave secondary arguments, detailed workflows, evaluation criteria, and feature inventories in the Blog or master content.
- End with a contextual CTA that follows naturally from the post.
- Sound confident and commercially relevant without unsupported hype.
- Sound like On-us speaking to the reader, using `we`, `our`, `at On-us`, `you`, or `your team` where natural.

## Output Rule For Repurpose Agent

When LinkedIn is requested, write the LinkedIn post under:

```text
### LinkedIn Clean Copy
```

The content inside `LinkedIn Clean Copy` must be clean publish-ready text:

- Do not include markdown heading symbols such as `#`, `##`, or `###`.
- Do not include "LinkedIn Draft / Notes", "Status", "Testing only", "Needs approval", or internal process comments inside the clean copy.
- Use plain hashtags such as `#SmartEVoucher`, not markdown links.
- Do not output LinkedIn URL markdown such as `[hashtag](...)`.
- If the source master content is not approved, keep warnings outside the clean copy under `Channel Review Notes`.
- The frontend will extract only this clean copy into an editable field.

## Recommended LinkedIn Format

Use a format similar to On-us' existing LinkedIn style:

```text
[Hook with a clear buyer insight, milestone, or market shift]

Short paragraph explaining why this matters.

Short paragraph connecting the issue to On-us' product, solution, case, or point of view.

[Optional topic-specific label]
• Benefit 1: short explanation
• Benefit 2: short explanation

Closing thought or CTA.

#RelevantHashtag #RelevantHashtag #RelevantHashtag
```

For announcement or milestone posts, the opening may be more celebratory.

For thought leadership or sales-angle posts, lead with the buyer problem or market signal first, then connect to On-us.

## Length Guidance

Follow On-us' existing LinkedIn style.

- Standard LinkedIn post: aim for around 140-190 words.
- Do not return fewer than 120 words unless the human explicitly requests a short caption.
- Milestone / partnership / trend post: no more than 220 words.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- Use 3-5 focused hashtags.

## Formatting Guidance

Allowed:

- Short hook in the first 1-2 lines.
- Use 0-2 topic-relevant emojis only when they improve navigation or energy.
- Choose emojis by subject and do not default to repeated `🔹` bullets.
- LinkedIn-native bold unicode headings may be used sparingly for section labels.
- Plain hashtags at the end.

Avoid:

- Markdown bold such as `**text**`.
- Markdown links for hashtags.
- Too many emojis or decorative symbols.
- Too many hashtags.
- Generic CTA only saying "Book a demo" without use-case context.
- Unsupported claim language such as "first", "only", "exclusive", "guaranteed", or "outperforms" unless approved in the source.
- Academic or meta-writing phrases such as "This piece argues", "This article explores", "The central thesis", or "It can be argued".
- Repeating the full Blog structure, FAQ, or every proof point in one post.
- Opening with a product definition when a buyer problem, market signal, or outcome would create a stronger marketing hook.
- Turning the post into consideration-stage collateral with detailed workflows, procurement criteria, long definitions, multiple proof points, FAQs, or caveat-heavy explanations.
- Em dashes, fragmented triplets, and staccato punchline fragments.
- `#Onus`, `hashtag#` artifacts, export markup, or placeholders.
- British English unless the human input or reviewer confirms an approved override.
- Reusing `How this benefits [audience]:` and the same generic CTA as the default structure for every post.

## Governance Gate

- Treat the Master Content Human Review Appendix and Reviewer Governance Handoff as restrictions, not copy ideas.
- Omit anything marked requires approval, confirm, pending, TBC, not approved, internal only, never use, avoid, unsupported, or open question.
- Do not add a statistic, named client, named partner, product capability, comparison, or market-leadership claim during repurposing.
- Apply the status rules in `on_us_intelligence.md` to every On-us Intelligence sentence. Keep roadmap capability explicitly future-facing.
