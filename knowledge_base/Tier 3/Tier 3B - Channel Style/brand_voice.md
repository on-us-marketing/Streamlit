# LinkedIn Brand Voice

Last updated: July 2026
Status: Draft for review
Use stage: LinkedIn repurposing only

## Purpose

Use this file to transform reviewed master content into an On-us LinkedIn post that feels lively, direct, human, and commercially relevant. For LinkedIn repurposing, this file is the highest-priority style reference. It should be read in full before drafting the post.

This file controls voice, rhythm, and presentation only. It is not a factual source. Never take product capabilities, client names, partner relationships, proof points, statistics, or approval status from historical LinkedIn examples or from this file. Those facts must remain within the approved master content and reviewer boundaries.

## Core Voice

On-us LinkedIn copy should sound like On-us speaking to a buyer, partner, or community member.

The voice is:

- Confident: state the useful point clearly.
- Human: use natural first-person language such as "we", "our", and "at On-us" where appropriate.
- Buyer-aware: address "you" or the named buyer team when it makes the message more relevant.
- Energetic: create momentum with active verbs, a strong hook, and selective visual emphasis.
- Practical: connect the idea to a real business decision, campaign, use case, or outcome.
- Warm: celebrate partners, people, and milestones without sounding ceremonial or inflated.
- Light to read: deliver one marketable idea that can be understood in one pass through the feed.

The voice is not:

- Academic or essay-like.
- Detached third-person narration about what "this piece" or "this article" does.
- Corporate jargon arranged into dense paragraphs.
- Hype built from unsupported superlatives.
- A shortened copy of the Blog.
- Consideration-stage collateral that explains the full solution, evaluation journey, or implementation logic.

## First 210 Characters

LinkedIn may truncate the post after roughly the first 210 characters. Put the single strongest, most quotable message in the opening 1-2 lines.

Strong hook types:

- Buyer insight: "Cross-border spend should be planned as a loyalty program, not just a campaign."
- Timely opportunity: "Mid Autumn is still months away, but early gifting decisions have already started."
- Question: "What turns a reward from a one-off benefit into a measurable engagement moment?"
- Milestone: "We're excited to bring [approved milestone] to market."
- Data-led signal: Start with one approved and attributed statistic, then explain why it matters.
- Human moment: "This week, our team met..." for talent, culture, or event content.

Do not spend the opening on background, a long definition, or "In today's landscape" filler. Do not hide the strongest sentence near the CTA.

## Preferred Language

Use active, concrete verbs where they accurately match the approved source:

- help
- build
- connect
- create
- deliver
- support
- strengthen
- expand
- simplify
- activate
- understand
- turn
- bring to market
- make visible
- plan
- explore

Useful On-us sentence starters:

- "At On-us, we're helping..."
- "Here's what this means for [buyer]..."
- "For [buyer], the opportunity is..."
- "We're excited to share..."
- "We're proud to support..."
- "The practical value is clear..."
- "That is where [approved On-us product] can support..."
- "Ready to explore [specific use case]?"
- "Talk to us about [specific buyer objective]."

Use these selectively. Do not repeat the same starter, section label, or CTA across every post.

## Sentence Style

- Prefer active voice and direct statements.
- Mix short and medium sentences for a conversational rhythm.
- Keep each paragraph to 1-3 sentences.
- Use "we" or "On-us" when describing the brand's role.
- Use "you" when the post can naturally address the buyer's decision.
- Keep technical explanation brief and connect it to a buyer outcome.
- Use one central message per post.
- Keep consideration-stage detail in the Blog or master content. LinkedIn should communicate one idea and one practical takeaway, not reproduce the complete evaluation journey.
- Use no more than one proof point and one short product-mechanism sentence.
- Use no more than two concise benefits when a list is genuinely useful.
- Leave feature inventories, detailed workflows, procurement criteria, strategic frameworks, FAQs, and secondary examples in the Blog or master content.

Never use:

- "This piece argues..."
- "This article offers..."
- "This article explores..."
- "The goal of this comparison..."
- "The central thesis is..."
- "It can be argued that..."
- Fragmented triplets such as "Infrequent. Hard to personalize. Even harder to measure."
- Repeated staccato fragments used as a punchline.
- Em dashes.

## Emoji Guidance

Use emojis as topic-specific navigation or tone cues, not decoration. Emoji choice should change with the subject instead of repeating a house template.

- Use 0-2 emojis in a standard post.
- Use no emoji when the topic is sensitive, formal, or strongly data-led.
- Use one opening emoji for a milestone or campaign when it fits.
- Use one consistent bullet marker for a benefits list.
- Do not place an emoji after every sentence.
- Do not use multiple unrelated emojis in the same line.
- Do not default to `🔹` in every post. Use plain bullets when no emoji adds meaning.
- Do not force an opening emoji. The hook should still work without one.

Suggested contextual options:

Choose only emojis relevant to the topic.

## Formatting

LinkedIn Clean Copy must be directly pasteable.

Recommended format:

```text
[Strong hook within the first 210 characters]

1-2 short paragraphs explaining why it matters to the target buyer.

[Optional short section label]
• Benefit or takeaway
• Benefit or takeaway

Closing point in the On-us voice.

Specific CTA.

#RelevantTag #RelevantTag #RelevantTag
```

Formatting rules:

- A Unicode-bold title or short section label may be used sparingly.
- Use at most two styled labels in one post.
- Keep visible paragraphs short.
- Use bullets only when they improve scanning.
- Use no more than two bullet points.
- Use 3-5 focused hashtags at the end.
- Use plain hashtags, not markdown links or `hashtag#` artifacts.
- Never use `#Onus`. A hashtag cannot preserve the approved On-us spelling.
- Do not use markdown headings, markdown bold, tables, reviewer notes, or placeholders.

## Structure Rotation

Choose the structure that fits the content. Do not make every post use "How this benefits [audience]:" followed by three bullets.

### Buyer Insight / POV

Hook -> buyer tension -> On-us point of view -> practical implication -> CTA.

### Market Signal / Data

Approved statistic or trend -> why it matters -> buyer action -> relevant On-us context -> source note / CTA.

### Product / Use Case

Buyer problem -> product mechanism -> 2-3 concrete uses or benefits -> CTA.

### Partnership / Milestone

News in the first line -> why it matters -> approved roles and value -> acknowledgement -> CTA.

### Event / Community / Talent

Human moment -> what happened -> useful takeaway -> On-us connection -> invitation or acknowledgement.

### Question-Led

Buyer question -> direct answer -> brief mechanism or example -> CTA.

## CTA Rotation

Make the CTA specific to the post. Suitable patterns include:

- "Explore how Smart E-Vouchers can support [specific campaign objective]."
- "Talk to us about [specific buyer use case]."
- "See how On-us supports [specific workflow]."
- "Read the full update: [approved URL]"
- "Meet the team at [approved event details]."
- "Ready to plan [specific initiative]? Let's talk."

Do not repeat "Talk to us about reward-led engagement for your next campaign cycle" across unrelated posts. Do not use a generic "Book a demo" when a more contextual CTA is available.

## Governance Inheritance

Repurposing cannot make a claim safer by shortening or rephrasing it.

- Anything marked `requires approval`, `confirm`, `pending`, `TBC`, `not approved`, `internal only`, `never use`, or `avoid` in the master content or reviewer output must stay out of LinkedIn Clean Copy.
- Do not introduce a new statistic, named client, named partner, product capability, comparative result, or market leadership claim.
- Do not turn an enterprise objective into a proven On-us result.
- Do not use comparative claims such as "outperforms cashback" unless the exact comparison is approved.
- Treat historical LinkedIn posts as voice references only, never evidence.
- Apply the current On-us Intelligence status from `on_us_intelligence.md`. Roadmap capabilities require future-facing language.

## Language Standard

Use American English by default:

- behavior
- analyze
- optimize
- program
- personalized
- fulfillment

Use another language standard only when the human input explicitly requests it or an approved co-branded asset requires it.

## Final Self-Check

Before returning LinkedIn Clean Copy, confirm:

1. The strongest line appears in the first 210 characters.
2. The copy sounds like On-us speaking, not an essay describing itself.
3. One central marketing message is clear.
4. Every claim remains inside the master and reviewer boundary.
5. Roadmap capability is not written as live capability.
6. There are no em dashes, fragmented triplets, banned words, or `#Onus`.
7. American English is used unless an explicit override applies.
8. The CTA and format fit this specific post rather than a repeated template.
9. The post reads as awareness / marketing content, not consideration-stage collateral.
10. Emoji choice reflects the topic and does not default to repeated `🔹` bullets.
